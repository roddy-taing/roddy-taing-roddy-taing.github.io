I am a Statistics Ph.D. student at the University of Wisconsin–Madison. I previously studied mathematics and physics at the University of Connecticut. Instead of continuing my physics education, I pursued a statistics PhD to work with scientists across many domains to help solve their problems.

## Research

### Statistical network analysis

My work centers around statistical network analysis. I examine how large networks can be represented as low-rank matrix plus some additional nuisance noise. My most recent ICML publication explores how robust certain network embedding methods are to model misspecification in the embedding dimension.

## Statistics Publications

- Roddy Taing and Keith Levin. [On the Effect of Misspecifying the Embedding Dimension in Low-rank Network Models](/files/8527-2.pdf). *International Conference on Machine Learning*, 2026.
